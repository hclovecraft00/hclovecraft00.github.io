It has been 5 days of me being in Ho Chi Minh, Vietnam! Let’s do a list of things I love

I love….

  • The casual religiosity in the everyday prevalent everywhere. Little shrines dotting the city and positioned smack-centre of store fronts. Joss sticks stuck in holes of cinderblocks and cracks of concrete pavements
  • Ca Phe……. There is no greater coffee genre than Vietnamese coffee. Strong, sweet, and goes down so easily. It’s so sinful. I liken it to fruit alcohol at Don Don Donki in terms of indulgence
  • Cables hanging overhead, though now cleaned and tidied up into neat bundles, a constant reminder of technological materiality. A physical manifestation of digital connectedness
  • Crossing roads. While often said to be the scariest part of Vietnam, I got used to it very quickly! Admittedly, the drivers here are not as hostile as the ones in NYC.
  • Grabbike. My favourite mode of transportation. Just so much fun to be on. Insane core training to balance. Very intimate too. I fall in love with my grabbikers a lot, there’s just something about a man going very fast and putting you into mildly life threatening situations
  • People telling me I look Vietnamese. It’s like if someone told a weaboo they look Japanese. I am a proud #vinaboo. I’ve been told four times now!
  • Groupthink and synergy of everyday commuters. There’s a secret telepathic language going on with bikers of Ho Chi Minh, where they simply know where the other is going at all times.
  • Cyclists. Those who persist in this mode of transportation despite all the tribulations
  • Shopping. Vietnam has truly become a powerhouse in fashion and design in Southeast Asia. It’s even better than China in my opinion
  • Dance culture in local clubs. While I’ve only been in one thus far, the music was pretty good. Goes from Kpop to Manyao to Vinahouse. Much better than Zouk where all they do is play remixes of Shakira and ROSE.
  • Dance culture pt.2, they also sync up! Very cutesy movements that everyone can do together. Feels like zumba at some point
  • The residential areas just look like my hometown GuTian in Fujian, China.
  • BROKEN RICE. Tiny tiny grains of rice with sweet and spicy honey drizzle… truly the best meal to ever exist in this world
